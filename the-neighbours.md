# The Neighbours

Kavita's job — one catalogue of your
book files, read anywhere — has older,
simpler answers, and one very capable
neighbour. These are characterisations
from the projects' own documentation and
this series' experience; the lab ran
Kavita only.

## The plain folder, and a desktop reader

The zero-server answer: a folder of
books on one machine, opened with a
desktop reader — Thorium or Foliate on
Linux, Books on a Mac, any of a dozen on
Windows — or just the file manager and
whatever opens on double-click. For one
reader, on one machine, this is honestly
enough, and it has real virtues: no
server to run, no accounts, the file is
right there, and a good desktop reader's
typography will beat any web reader.

What the folder cannot do is everything
that made you want a server: the same
shelf on every device, progress that
follows you, per-person libraries,
covers and summaries gathered into one
browsable catalogue, new books appearing
for everyone the moment they land on the
disk. Kavita is what the folder grows
into when the collection, the devices,
or the readers multiply past one. The
books are the same files either way —
nothing you shelve in Kavita is locked
away from the folder, which is why
starting small costs nothing.

## Calibre: the workbench

Calibre (which has its own guide in this
series) is the elder statesman of ebook
management, and the relationship is
complementary more than competitive.
Calibre is a *workbench*: it edits
metadata seriously, converts between
formats — the EPUB a PDF wants to be,
chapter 7 — repairs and polishes files,
and manages a library as a craftsman
manages stock. It has a content server
of its own, but its centre of gravity is
the desk, not the household.

The usual division of labour: **Calibre
prepares the library; Kavita serves
it.** Fix the metadata in Calibre, let
Kavita's scanner read the corrected
files (chapter 5's rule 3 is why that
works — the metadata lives in the
files), and read through Kavita's
shelves and OPDS door. A reader who only
converts and sideloads to one e-reader
may never need Kavita; a household that
reads everywhere may keep Calibre on one
machine as the workshop and never open
it to read.

## The OPDS apps: doors, not rivals

The reader apps of chapter 10 — the
library and e-reader apps that speak
OPDS — are not alternatives to Kavita
but its clients. Choosing between them
is choosing a reading room, not a
library: the shelves behind the door are
the same. That is OPDS working as
intended, and it is the reason this
guide can bless whichever app you
already like without a comparison table.
